Quick reference

How to run it.

Supplied policy documents become a clause-by-clause extract keyed to a proposal: verbatim requirement, modal verb preserved, force classified, evidence named. Extracts requirements; never judges compliance.

Run it

Attach the policy documents and the scheme material, and say the stage:

Pull the relevant policies out of this local plan for a 42-unit scheme on the allocated site.
Policy extract for the DAS — here's the local plan, the design code and our drawings.
Which of these policies actually bite at 9 units? Show me the thresholds.
What's in the plan that our current pack says nothing about?

Give it unit count, floor area, height and use class. Thresholds decide whether a policy applies at all, and the skill will not guess them.

The extract

PartWhat it holds
Document registerEach policy document, its adoption status, date and plan period — as the document itself states them.
Extract tableRef · Policy number · Verbatim requirement · Force · Topic · Applies because · Evidence that would demonstrate it · Where the scheme answers it.
Cross-referencesEvery SPD, design code or standard a policy points to, and whether it was supplied.
Unanswered listRequirements no supplied scheme material addresses. Requirements first.
Weight & currencyAdopted vs emerging, and anything whose status the documents leave unclear.

Force — the column that does the work:

ForceSignals
requirement"must", "shall", "will only be permitted where"
strong expectation"should", "will be expected to", "normally" — record the exception wording too
aspiration"encouraged", "sought", "where possible" — not a test
conditional"where", "if" — the trigger is recorded separately
illustrativediagrams, "for example", indicative layouts — explicitly not a requirement

Policy documents braid a hard requirement, a conditional and an aspiration into one paragraph. The expensive mistake is not missing a policy — it is flattening one. Each requirement becomes its own row, with its own verb.

What it will not do — by design

  • Judge compliance. No "complies", "meets", "fails", or any synonym, on any row. It states what is required and where the scheme speaks to it.
  • Predict a decision or characterise an officer's likely view.
  • Supply a policy or figure from memory. A document you did not send is on the cross-reference list as missing, not in the extract.
  • Soften or harden a modal verb. "Should" never becomes "must". "Normally" never disappears.
  • Treat an illustrative diagram as a requirement, however prescriptive the layout looks.
  • Assign weight to an emerging policy. It records the stage; weight is a planning judgement.
  • Assess adequacy. "The DAS section 4 addresses this" is a fact. "Adequately addresses" is a judgement and never appears.

Work the output

  1. Check Applies because on every row before designing to anything — a threshold policy that does not bite is a requirement you do not have.
  2. Chase the unsupplied cross-references first. If four policies point to a design code nobody sent, the scheme's real requirements are in that document.
  3. Give the unanswered list to whoever is writing the DAS or planning statement; it is the contents page.
  4. Take every [force unclear] and [to verify] row to the planning consultant as a question, not a decision.
  5. Re-run on the emerging plan separately, and keep the two extracts apart. Merging adopted and emerging policy is how weight gets lost.
  6. Date the extract and re-run it before submission. Policy moves.

Remember: the extract is a skeleton for the planning statement, not the statement. Every judgement it declines to make is a judgement someone qualified still has to take — the extract's job is to make sure they can see exactly what they are judging.

Triggering

When Claude reaches for it.

This is the description Claude itself reads to decide whether the skill applies. It is worth knowing, because it tells you exactly how to phrase the ask — and what the skill will decline.

planning-policy-extract

Turn supplied planning policy documents — local plan, neighbourhood plan, SPD, design code, site allocation, committee report — into a clause-by-clause policy extract keyed to a proposal: policy reference, verbatim requirement, whether it is a requirement or an aspiration, what evidence would demonstrate it, and what the scheme must answer. Use this WHENEVER someone supplies policy documents and asks to "pull out the relevant policies", "what does the local plan require here", "build a policy compliance table", "what do we need to answer in the DAS", "policy extract for this site", "which policies apply to this scheme", or wants a planning statement's policy skeleton. Do NOT use it to decide whether a scheme complies (it extracts requirements, never judges them), to research what policy applies (that needs the documents supplied — for pipeline sweeps use planning-pipeline-research), to write the planning statement prose, or to give planning advice or predict a decision.
