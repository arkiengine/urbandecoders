Quick reference

How to run it.

Incoming RFIs become a managed log plus drafts for the ones already answered by the issued documents. Triage instrument, not a contract administrator. Never answers from memory, never instructs, never approves.

Run it

Attach the RFIs and the issued documents they refer to:

Log and triage this week's RFIs.
Which of these are actually variations or substitutions? Don't draft those.
Draft responses for anything already answered in the issued drawings.
Six RFIs on the same detail — what's the underlying problem?

Without the issued documents it can classify but cannot tell an information request from a design gap — and it will say so rather than guess.

The five things in an RFI inbox

They look identical and are not:

TypeSignalHandling
informationThe answer exists in issued documents.Draft a response citing the source.
design gapNo issued document answers it.Route to the designer. No draft.
substitution"Confirm acceptability", "equal and approved".Never drafted. An approval decision with specifier consequences.
instruction sought"Confirm we should proceed with…"Never drafted. A prose answer may be an instruction.
record buildingRestates a delay, a late issue or a change.Never drafted. Flag to the contract administrator.
unclearCannot be classified from what was supplied.Query. Never guessed.

Where an RFI carries two types, the higher-consequence one governs. A substitution dressed as an information request is handled as a substitution, always.

The output

PartWhat it holds
RFI logRef · Date · From · Subject · Type · Element · Drawing/clause · Owner · Status · Date required · Days outstanding · Exposure.
Triage summaryCounts by type, with items that must not be answered without a human decision listed first.
Draft responsesOnly for information items. Each cites sheet, clause, revision and date. Each marked draft for review.
Exposure registerItems carrying cost, time, approval or scope consequence — consequence named, never quantified.
Pattern noteWhere a cluster of RFIs points at one underlying problem.
Query listWhat cannot be answered from the supplied documents, and what each needs.

A drafted response has four parts and nothing else: the question restated in one line · the answer stated plainly · the source with revision and date · draft for review — not issued. No "as you know", no "we would suggest", no "please proceed on the basis that". Each of those is a route to an instruction nobody priced.

What it will not do — by design

  • Answer from memory or general construction knowledge. A plausible, correct-sounding answer that is not in the issued documents is the worst output this skill could produce.
  • Issue anything. Every draft is a draft.
  • Approve a substitution or express a view on an alternative product.
  • Instruct. No "proceed", no "confirmed", no phrasing readable as either.
  • Assess entitlement to time or money, estimate delay, or rate a claim's strength.
  • Interpret a contract clause. The item is routed with the term named and nothing more.
  • Renumber the contractor's references. The log must speak their language or the correspondence stops matching.
  • Soften a record building RFI into an information request because it is awkward.

Work the output

  1. Read the triage summary before any draft. The items listed first are the ones that cause damage when answered casually.
  2. Check every information draft against the cited sheet yourself before issuing. The citation is the value; confirm it.
  3. Route substitutions to whoever holds specification authority — answering "yes" is an approval with professional consequences.
  4. Give record building items to the contract administrator the day they arrive, not at the end of the cycle.
  5. Act on the pattern note. Six RFIs on one junction is one detailing problem, and answering them individually guarantees a seventh.
  6. Watch the days-outstanding column. Late responses are the raw material of the claim the log is quietly recording.

Remember: the log is a record that will be read adversarially one day. Every classification quotes the sentence that produced it, so the triage can be defended three months later by someone who was not there.

Triggering

When Claude reaches for it.

This is the description Claude itself reads to decide whether the skill applies. It is worth knowing, because it tells you exactly how to phrase the ask — and what the skill will decline.

rfi-log-builder

Turn incoming RFIs — emails, portal exports, a contractor's spreadsheet — into a managed RFI log and a draft response pack: each RFI classified by what it actually is (information request, design gap, substitution, instruction in disguise, or a variation claim wearing a question mark), routed to the right responder, with the contractual and programme exposure flagged and a response drafted only where the supplied documents already answer it. Use this WHENEVER someone supplies RFIs and asks to "log these RFIs", "triage the RFIs", "draft RFI responses", "which of these are actually variations", "build an RFI register", "what are the contractor really asking", "turn these emails into a log", or runs a weekly contract administration cycle. Do NOT use it to answer an RFI whose answer is not already in the supplied documents (it drafts from evidence, never from memory), to issue instructions, to assess entitlement to time or money, or to give contractual or legal advice.
