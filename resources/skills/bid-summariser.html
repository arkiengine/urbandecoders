Quick reference

How to run it.

Turns an ITT / RFP / tender invitation into a one-sheet internal bid summary: dates, submission requirements, likely scope by RIBA stage, easy misses, conflicts to clarify, fee position, go/no-go inputs. Not the proposal — the sheet the team reads before deciding to write one.

Run it

Attach the tender pack (every file — appendices and forms matter most) and ask:

Summarise this bid request for the team.
Summarise this ITT — what's the deadline and what do we need to submit?
Check this tender for catches and conflicts before our go/no-go.
Addendum 1 attached — what changed against the earlier summary?      (delta mode)

Attach fee benchmarks / rate cards too if you want an evidenced fee range — without them the fee section is deliberately TBC.

The summary

SectionWhat it holds
At-a-glanceClient, ask, deadline (earliest, conflict-flagged), fit H/M/L, decision needed by when.
The opportunity3–5 bullets on what this is and why it might suit.
Key datesTable with source clause per date. Conflicting dates shown as separate CONFLICT rows.
Submission requirementsWhat, format, limits, channel, mandatory forms. Conflicts flagged inline.
Scope by stageRIBA-stage table, every line [stated §ref] or [inferred: reason]. Scope-leak called out.
EvaluationCriteria, weightings — summed and checked — interview arrangements.
Risks & easy missesChecklist-driven watch-outs, each with a clause reference.
Conflicts & clarificationsNumbered, verbatim fragments both sides, drafted questions, question deadline.
Fee positionEvidenced range + assumptions, or Fee: TBC + what's needed. Missing info listed here.

Tags — trust nothing untagged: [stated §x] the pack says it · [inferred: …] follows from something stated · TBC not evidenced · [to verify] standard/policy named, figure not recited · CONFLICT documents disagree.

Three deadlines in one pack is normal. The summary plans around the earliest and says so — it never quietly picks one.

What it will not do — by design

  • Write the response. No method statements, no win themes.
  • Resolve conflicts. Both versions cited; question drafted; you decide what to ask.
  • Invent fees or dates. No benchmarks attached → fee TBC, with the list of what to attach.
  • Quote standards from memory. Space standards and policy figures are named to check, [to verify].
  • Make the go/no-go call. Fit rating + decision-needed line; the call is the practice's.
  • Web-search the client or site unless you ask; no client names in output filenames.

Work the output

  1. Key dates first — diary the clarification deadline and any site-visit booking deadline today; they expire before the tender does.
  2. Read Conflicts & clarifications with a director; submit the drafted questions through the stated route well before the cut-off.
  3. Hand Submission requirements to whoever owns the document — page-limit arithmetic and portal registration are their first jobs.
  4. Take Scope by stage + Fee position to the go/no-go: every [inferred] and scope-leak line is a pricing decision.
  5. Request every "available on request" document immediately — pricing waits on them.
  6. When an addendum lands, re-run in delta mode; never overwrite the earlier summary.

Remember: the summary is a strong first pass, not a substitute for the bid manager reading the conditions of tender. Spot-check the dates against the pack yourself. Test drive: run it on the Foundry Lane sample ITT and score against its defect key.

Triggering

When Claude reaches for it.

This is the description Claude itself reads to decide whether the skill applies. It is worth knowing, because it tells you exactly how to phrase the ask — and what the skill will decline.

bid-summariser

Turn a bid request — ITT, RFP, RFQ, tender invitation, EOI, SQ/PQQ or framework mini-competition for architectural services — into a scannable internal bid summary: at-a-glance verdict, key dates, submission requirements, likely scope by RIBA stage, easy misses, conflicts to clarify, fee position, and the go/no-go decision needed. Use this WHENEVER a user supplies tender documents (.pdf, .docx, portal text, pasted email) and asks to "summarise this bid", "summarise this ITT/RFP/tender", "what are they asking for", "is this worth bidding", "what's the deadline and what do we need to submit", "brief the team on this opportunity", or "help us decide go/no-go". Also trigger on "what could we miss in this submission" or "check this tender for catches". Do NOT use for writing the response itself (that is rfp-response-builder, which runs after this), for turning a client design brief into requirements, or for reviewing the eventual appointment terms.
