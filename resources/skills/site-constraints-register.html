At a glance

What it takes, what you get.

It takes

Title plans, searches, constraint reports, utility records, planning history, survey notes

You get

One row per constraint with its source document, extent and confidence tag — plus an outstanding-searches list at the front, a conflicts log, and a summary of which project decisions cannot safely be taken yet.

It will not

Source constraints or produce one from memory; advise on title, legal or contamination matters; declare a site developable.

Quick reference

How to run it.

A bundle of site evidence becomes one traceable register: constraint, source, extent, confidence, and the decision it blocks. Registers what you supply — never sources constraints, never from memory.

Run it

Attach the site documents and say what decision is coming:

Build a constraints register from these — we're deciding whether to bid.
Constraints register for this site, Stage 1. What searches are we missing?
We have the title pack and a Phase 1 desktop study. What can we not yet decide?
Add this ecology walkover to the register and tell me what it closes.   (iteration)

Naming the decision sharpens the "what it affects" and "decisions blocked" columns. No answer? It runs against a generic feasibility decision set.

The register

PartWhat it holds
Source inventoryEvery file received: what it is, who wrote it, date, revision, and the status it claims for itself.
Outstanding searchesFirst, before any constraint row. What has not been looked at and what each would settle.
Register rowsRef · Constraint · Category · Extent · Source + location · Evidence tag · What it affects · Status.
Conflicts logTwo sources disagreeing on the same constraint — both quoted, neither resolved.
Decisions blockedWhich project decisions cannot be taken safely yet, each naming the rows that block it.

Evidence tags — read no row without checking its tag:

TagMeaning
[documented]Stated in a supplied document; page or section named.
[surveyed]Measured on site or in a supplied survey.
[indicative]Source calls itself desktop or indicative. The extent is not reliable.
[reported]Someone said so. Not yet evidenced.
[assumed]Carried forward to keep the register usable; basis stated.
[to verify]Named by a source that cannot establish it; says what would.

Categories: Title & legal · Planning designation · Ecology & trees · Flood & water · Ground & contamination · Utilities & services · Access & highways · Heritage & archaeology · Neighbouring & rights · Infrastructure & safeguarding · Other.

A thin pack should produce a short register and a long outstanding-searches list. That is the correct answer, not a failure. Padding it with plausible constraints is the exact failure this skill exists to prevent.

What it will not do — by design

  • Source anything. No searching for flood zones, listings, TPOs or planning history; no constraint from memory. It registers supplied evidence and names the searches that would produce the rest.
  • Advise. Covenants, easements and contamination are recorded as what the documents say, with the profession that must advise named.
  • Declare a site clear. The absence of a row is the absence of evidence, never the absence of a constraint.
  • Dimension an indicative extent. A shaded area on an unscaled plan never becomes a metre figure.
  • Merge two sources into one tidy row. The tidiness is the lie.
  • Promote a [reported] row because it is probably true.
  • Quote a zone, policy or clause from memory — the document gets named and marked [to verify].

Work the output

  1. Read the outstanding-searches list before the register. It is the commissioning list.
  2. Send the conflicts log to whoever issued the documents — those are their contradictions to rule on, not yours.
  3. Use decisions blocked to decide what the next fee stage can honestly cover.
  4. Close [reported] and [assumed] rows before any of them touches a design decision; the register tracks when each flipped and on what evidence.
  5. Re-run it as searches land. The register is a living baseline, and each addition should visibly shorten the gaps list.
  6. File the source inventory with the register — it dates the whole thing. A register is only as current as its newest document.

Remember: a short, honest register beats a long, confident one. The row nobody could evidence is worth more to you visible than tidied away.

Triggering

When Claude reaches for it.

This is the description Claude itself reads to decide whether the skill applies. It is worth knowing, because it tells you exactly how to phrase the ask — and what the skill will decline.

site-constraints-register

Turn a bundle of site evidence — title plans, searches, constraint reports, utility records, planning history, survey notes, consultant memos — into a single traceable constraints register: one row per constraint, each with its source document, extent, confidence tag and the decision it blocks, plus a gaps list naming the searches not yet done. Use this WHENEVER someone supplies site documents and asks to "build a constraints register", "what are the constraints on this site", "site due diligence", "pull the constraints together", "what's stopping us building here", "red flags on this site", "what do we still need to find out", or wants a Stage 0–1 constraints baseline before a feasibility or offer. Do NOT use it to source constraint data from the web or from memory (it registers only what you supply — for sourced planning pipeline work use planning-pipeline-research), to assess planning policy compliance (planning-policy-extract), to plot layers on a map (gis-interactive-map), or to give legal, title or environmental advice.
