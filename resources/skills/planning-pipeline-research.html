Quick reference

How to run it.

Sweeps the live UK planning pipeline for a place or a site and returns sourced, comparable scheme profiles — ten fixed parameters, a citation and access date on every line. Not a market overview — an auditable register extract.

Run it

Research the planning pipeline for large schemes in [city/borough].
What's submitted or consented within 500m of [site address]?     (site-context mode)
Track applications by [developer] across [authorities].           (applicant sweep)
Deep dive on [scheme name] — status, history, design team.        (named-scheme mode)
Give me the comparison table as an xlsx for the pipeline tracker.

Needs live web access to registers and portals. Without it you get a search plan — which LPAs, portals and terms to run — never a pipeline from memory.

Profiling the developers rather than the schemes is the sibling bd-market-research skill. Run the pipeline first, then profile the applicants it surfaces.

The deliverable

SectionWhat it holds
Scope noteGeography → the actual LPA list, "large" threshold, stages in/out, purpose, as-at date, and every clarification or correction made to the request. Read first.
Method notePortals searched, terms used, dates accessed, what was down. Makes the run repeatable — without it the report is just assertions.
Pipeline summary3–6 bullets: counts by status, dominant uses, busiest authorities, design-team patterns.
Project profilesOne per scheme, ten parameters in order: location · applicant · use & scale · status · reference & LPA · key dates · design team · strategic context · stated value · risks & signals. Every line tagged.
Comparison tableScheme × LPA / ref / use / scale / status / architect, for a 30-second scan.
Gaps & caveatsPress-only schemes, unverified leads, portals not reached, and the standing re-verify warning.
Declined itemsAnything in the request the skill won't do, said openly rather than dropped.

Tags — an untagged fact doesn't ship:

TagMeaning
[register: LPA, ref, accessed]Read on the official application register. Gold standard.
[app docs: doc, ref] · [committee: LPA, date]From the application file or committee papers.
[developer: source, date] · [press: outlet, date]Published, promotional or journalistic. A lead, not a verification.
[not found]Searched, not established — and where you looked. A real finding.
[to verify]A policy figure, lapse date or referral threshold for you to confirm at source.

Status precision — the words that cost credibility

Never writeWrite instead
"Approved" (resolution to grant)Resolution to grant subject to s106, [date]; decision notice not issued as at [date]
"Approved" (validated)Validated [date], pending consideration — an administrative act, not a view
"Consented" (old permission)Granted [date]; commencement [not found]; whether extant [to verify]
"Consented" (outline)Outline granted; reserved matters [which] outstanding
"About to start" (s106 unsigned)Permission granted; s106 unsigned / pre-commencement conditions undischarged — not implementable as at [date]
"In planning" (consultation website)Consulting ahead of an intended submission; no application on the register as at [date]

Full ladder: references/status-and-sources.md.

What it will not do — by design

  • Invent or reconstruct references. Refs are transcribed from a retrieved page, never pattern-matched from a sequence.
  • Estimate values, areas or unit counts. Not stated in a source means [not found], even when asked to fill the gap.
  • Predict outcomes. No approval odds, no "likely to be consented", no scoring schemes as winnable. Report objections, consultee positions and committee history; the judgement is yours.
  • Present press as register fact, or let a marketing quantum overwrite the application documents.
  • Convert or normalise floorspace. GIA/GEA/NIA and sq m/sq ft are reported as given, mismatches flagged.
  • Research your own client or their landholdings for "context" — excluded, and said so. No client names in filenames or search queries.
  • Contact anyone. No case officers, agents, consultees; no comments, FOI or pre-app enquiries. Desk research on public records.
  • Trade in gossip. Observable facts only — a change of architect as shown on the documents, never why.
  • Quote policy from memory. Thresholds, determination and lapse periods are named to the document and marked [to verify].

Work the output

  1. Scope note first — check the LPA list is the geography you meant, and read the corrections.
  2. Method note second — a thin report with a full method note is a finding; a thin report without one is an unfinished job.
  3. Scan the comparison table, then read in full only what you'll actually speak to.
  4. Before any scheme goes in a deck or a client meeting, re-open its register entry: statuses were true on the access date, not today.
  5. Treat [press: …] rows as leads to confirm, and empty design-team slots as the BD signal they are.
  6. Feed site-context runs into the Stage 0–1 appraisal as emerging context — flag consented neighbours, don't design to them.

Remember: the ladder rung is the whole product. "Resolution to grant" and "approved" differ by an unsigned s106 and, sometimes, by years. Test drive: run it on the Caldergate sample request and score against its defect key.

Triggering

When Claude reaches for it.

This is the description Claude itself reads to decide whether the skill applies. It is worth knowing, because it tells you exactly how to phrase the ask — and what the skill will decline.

planning-pipeline-research

Research the live UK planning pipeline — large schemes at pre-application, submitted, under consideration, resolution to grant, consented or reserved matters — in a named city, borough or around a site, and return sourced, comparable scheme profiles with a citation and access date on every fact. Use this WHENEVER someone asks "what's in planning in [place]", "research the planning pipeline", "who's developing what in [city]", "find large schemes in planning", "what's been submitted or consented near this site", "site context — what's planned around here", "track applications by [developer]", or wants a pipeline sweep for BD, bid research or Stage 0–1 context. Requires live access to planning portals and registers — it must never produce a pipeline from memory. Do NOT use for drafting planning applications, assessing whether the user's own scheme complies with policy, legal or viability advice, or contacting officers. It profiles schemes, not companies — for developer organisations use bd-market-research.
