Quick reference

How to run it.

Turns a long report (feasibility study, D&AS, options appraisal, condition survey, viability study) into a one-page executive summary in the house style, plus a "for author review" checklist. Not a précis machine — a summary that stays exactly as certain as its report.

Run it

Attach the full report (PDF / Word / pasted text — every appendix) and ask:

Write the executive summary for this report.
Summarise this for the board — one page.                    (standard, default)
Give me the short version for the covering email.            (short, ≈150 words)
Extended summary with sub-headings for the funder pack.      (extended, ≈800 words)
Check the existing exec summary against the body.            (divergence check)

Optionally attach one or two past summaries or the practice style guide — they override the default house style.

The deliverable

PartWhat it holds
Framing lineReport title + revision, subject, audience, chosen length. Says exactly which document was summarised.
Purpose & scopeWhat was assessed, by whom, to what RIBA stage, on what evidential basis.
Key findingsThe facts a senior reader must have — hedges and figure bases intact.
RecommendationsThe report's recommendations, conditional where the report is conditional.
For author reviewNumbered checklist with locators: inferences, unreconciled figures, hedges preserved, documents not seen, [to verify] items. Never trimmed for length.

The two safeguards — check them before anything else:

SafeguardWhat it looks like in the output
Caveats survive"appears capable of consent, subject to…" stays hedged; "visual survey only" travels with the structural conclusion; informal officer advice is labelled informal; scenario-dependent viability names the scenario.
Numbers never reconciled silently"46 homes (Appendix A) against 48 (Section 5.2), unreconciled"; every figure carries its basis (incl./excl. contingency, GIA/NIA); derived figures labelled "derived".

A summary whose review checklist is long is working, not failing. The dangerous summary is the clean one-pager with nothing for the author to check.

What it will not do — by design

  • Upgrade certainty. "Punchier" sharpens prose, never claims — the certainty of a claim belongs to the report author.
  • Pick between conflicting figures. Both quoted, both located, handed to the author.
  • Add external facts. Nothing outside the attached report; standards figures are named and marked [to verify], never recalled from memory.
  • Declare verdicts. No consent, compliance or viability verdict stronger than the report's own words.
  • Summarise unseen material. "Issued separately" appendices are listed as not reviewed.
  • Copy the report's own exec summary. It writes fresh from the body and flags where the existing summary diverges.
  • Web-search the client or site unless asked; no client names or headline figures in filenames.
  • Sign off. Everything is a draft until the report author reviews it.

Work the output

  1. Read the For author review list first — it is the 2-minute route through everything contentious.
  2. Chase every unreconciled figure back to source and fix the report, not just the summary.
  3. Decide the hedges: only you can move "appears capable" to "will" — and only by closing the condition.
  4. Confirm the revision in the framing line matches what you are about to issue.
  5. Only then read the summary as prose and tune the voice.

Remember: the summary is a strong draft, not a sign-off — the checklist exists because the author decides what the practice puts its name to. Test drive: run it on the Ketteridge Maltings sample report and score against its defect key.

Triggering

When Claude reaches for it.

This is the description Claude itself reads to decide whether the skill applies. It is worth knowing, because it tells you exactly how to phrase the ask — and what the skill will decline.

executive-summary-builder

Turn a long technical report — feasibility study, design and access statement, options appraisal, condition survey, planning or heritage statement, viability study — into a one-page executive summary in the practice's house style, with every caveat preserved, every conflicting figure flagged rather than silently reconciled, and a "for author review" checklist so the author can sign it off in minutes. Use this WHENEVER an architect or project director attaches a report (.pdf, .docx or pasted text) and asks to "summarise this", "write the exec summary", "draft the executive summary section", "give me a one-pager for the board", "distil this for the client", "what does this report actually say", or "shorten this to a page". Also trigger when an existing executive summary needs rewriting or checking against the body. Do NOT use for processing a client brief into requirements, for writing or extending the report itself, for reviewing a design, or for minuting meetings.
