Quick reference

How to run it.

Correspondence becomes a change register: every candidate change traced to the exact wording that created it, with its real instruction status. Records what was said and by whom. Never values, never determines, never advises.

Run it

Attach the correspondence, minutes and any existing instruction register:

Build a change log from this correspondence — we think things have been instructed verbally.
What's been changed with no instruction behind it?
Reconstruct the change history for the cladding from these emails and minutes.
Which items need regularising in writing, and who by?

Say whose correspondence you have and whose you do not. A change register with a known blind spot is useful; one with an unknown blind spot is dangerous.

The log

PartWhat it holds
Change logRef · Date · Element · What changed · How it arose · Source + location · Verbatim trigger · Instruction status · Instructed by · Regularisation needed.
Verbal & implied listThe items most likely to cause a dispute. First.
Regularisation listWhat needs confirming in writing, by whom, referring to what.
ChronologyDate order — how a change history is reconstructed and how gaps become obvious.
Conflicts logTwo sources describing one change differently, or an instruction apparently contradicted later.
Query listMay be changes, cannot be established from the supplied material.

How it arose: client request · design development · site condition · statutory · contractor proposal · error correction · unknown.

Instruction status — the column nobody keeps:

StatusMeaning
formalA written instruction exists, with reference and date.
written non-formalConfirmed in writing but not as a contract instruction.
verbal recordedSaid verbally, captured in minutes or a site record.
verbal unrecordedReferred to as having been said, with no record of the saying.
impliedNo instruction at all — a response or approval that may operate as one.
none foundThe change happened with nothing instructing it.

implied is why this skill exists. "Yes, that works." "Happy with that." "Go ahead and we'll sort the paperwork." Each may have created a change, and each is invisible in a register built from formal instructions alone.

What it will not do — by design

  • Value anything. No cost, no quantities, no "minor", no "significant".
  • Assess entitlement to time or money, or whether a claim would succeed.
  • Decide whether something is a variation under the contract. It records what happened and what was said; the contract administrator determines.
  • Draft or issue an instruction.
  • Interpret contract clauses or advise on contractual position.
  • Promote a verbal unrecorded item because it is probably true, or demote an implied one because the wording was casual. Casual wording is the point.
  • Paraphrase a trigger. Verbatim or nothing — a paraphrase is worthless the moment anybody disagrees, and people disagree about exactly these sentences.
  • Attribute motive or characterise anyone's conduct.
  • Guess an origin. unknown is a real value.

Work the output

  1. Read the blind-spot note first. If you only have the architect's inbox, everything agreed client-to-contractor is missing.
  2. Work the regularisation list immediately. It is the whole reason to run this before a dispute rather than during one.
  3. Check the implied rows personally — each is a sentence someone in your team wrote, and only they know what they meant by it.
  4. Give the chronology to the contract administrator. Gaps in a date sequence are where the missing correspondence is.
  5. Close non-changes in the log with the reason rather than deleting them. The record that something was considered and dismissed is worth having.
  6. Re-run monthly. Reconstructing eighteen months of correspondence under pressure is the expensive version of this exercise.

Remember: the verbatim trigger column is the entire evidential value of the log. Everything else can be rebuilt; a sentence nobody quoted at the time cannot.

Triggering

When Claude reaches for it.

This is the description Claude itself reads to decide whether the skill applies. It is worth knowing, because it tells you exactly how to phrase the ask — and what the skill will decline.

variation-instruction-log

Turn project correspondence — emails, minutes, site records, RFI responses, marked drawings — into a change and instruction log: every item that may constitute a change identified, classified by how it arose, traced to the exact wording that created it, with its instruction status recorded and verbal or implied instructions flagged for regularisation. Use this WHENEVER someone asks to "log the variations", "what changes have we had", "build a change register", "did anyone instruct this", "find the verbal instructions", "track the instructions this month", "what's been changed without an instruction", or is reconstructing a change history. Do NOT use it to value a change, assess time or cost entitlement, decide whether something is a variation under the contract, issue or draft an instruction, or give contractual or legal advice — it records what was said and by whom, and names what needs regularising.
