Quick reference

How to run it.

Turns a meeting transcript (Teams/Zoom export, dictated notes, pasted text) into draft minutes: every action with owner and date, decisions separated from discussions, and a flag sheet for the project lead — verbal instructions, scope creep, contradictions, unowned actions, figures quoted from memory. Not a recap — a commercial safety net.

Run it

Attach the transcript (.md / .docx / .pdf / pasted text) and ask:

Minutes and actions from this transcript.
Did the client instruct anything in this call? Check for scope changes.
Here are DTM 13's minutes too — carry forward the open actions.        (carry-forward mode)

The minutes

SectionWhat it holds
Header + statusProject, meeting no., date, stage. Always marked DRAFT — for review before issue.
AttendancePresent / apologies / joined late / spoke-but-not-listed. Mismatches flagged.
SummaryFive bullets, maximum. Deliberately the smallest section.
Decisions (D-nn)Resolved positions only, with who made them and transcript refs. Reversals shown: final position stands, both refs kept.
Actions (A-nn)Every action, however small. Owner and due date as stated; TBC where not; inferred dates show their working.
Discussed — no decisionTopics that must not masquerade as settled, with where each was left.
Flags for the project leadVI verbal instruction · SC scope/fee · CO contradiction · UA unowned action · TV figure [to verify] · PR programme/stage · AU authority unclear. Verbatim quotes, transcript refs.
To confirmThe chase list: TBC owners/dates, inaudibles, actions given to absentees.

A short Decisions table and a long Discussed section is usually the accurate result. The dangerous minutes are the ones where every conversation reads as settled.

What it will not do — by design

  • Invent owners or dates. No name was taken → owner TBC + UA flag. "Obviously the engineer's job" is not the transcript.
  • Promote discussions to decisions. When in doubt it's discussed, with an action to decide.
  • Resolve contradictions. £40k at 00:26 and £60k at 00:58 both go in, referenced. Your call, made knowingly.
  • Paraphrase verbal instructions. The exact words are the evidence — quoted verbatim, flagged for written confirmation. It won't issue a CoI unless you ask for a draft.
  • Quote or "correct" regulations from memory. Figures cited in the meeting are recorded and marked [to verify] with the document named.
  • Fill in inaudibles. [inaudible — figure not captured] goes to the chase list, never guessed from context.
  • Sanitise. Awkward statements are minuted, attributed, referenced. Softening is your editorial decision at issue.
  • Web-search the client, site or attendees, or put client names in filenames.
  • Sign itself off. Everything is draft until the project lead reviews and issues it.

Work the output

  1. Read Flags first, not the summary — the VI/SC entries are where the money is. Decide today what needs written confirmation.
  2. Sweep Actions for TBC owners and dates; assign or chase while the meeting is fresh.
  3. Check Discussed — no decision against what you think was agreed. Anything you believed settled that appears here needs closing out.
  4. Resolve CO contradictions with the people who made them, then correct the draft.
  5. Confirm actions given to people who weren't in the room — they haven't accepted anything yet.
  6. Edit, then issue as your own minutes. The draft status line comes off only when you take ownership of the record.

Remember: the skill works only from the transcript — it cannot know what was agreed off-call or in the room before recording started. Spot-check attribution against your own memory of the meeting before issue. Test drive: run it on the Osier Works DTM 14 sample transcript and score against its defect key.

Triggering

When Claude reaches for it.

This is the description Claude itself reads to decide whether the skill applies. It is worth knowing, because it tells you exactly how to phrase the ask — and what the skill will decline.

meeting-summariser

Turn a meeting transcript (Teams/Zoom export, dictated notes, or pasted text) into draft minutes for an architecture practice: a complete action list with named owners and dates, decisions separated from discussions, and a flag sheet for the project lead covering verbal client instructions, scope creep, contradictions, unowned actions and figures quoted from memory. Use this WHENEVER someone supplies a transcript or notes from a design team meeting (DTM), project or client meeting (PCM), consultant coordination workshop, client briefing call or internal design review and asks for "minutes", "meeting notes", "notes and actions", "actions from this meeting", "minute this", "who agreed to what", "what did we decide", or "write this up for issue". Also trigger on "did the client instruct anything in this call" or "check this transcript for scope changes". Do NOT use for site visit write-ups from photos, for drafting agendas, or for summarising documents that are not records of a meeting.
