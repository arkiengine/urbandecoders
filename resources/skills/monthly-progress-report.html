Install

Install just this one.

Two routes. Both end with Claude loading the skill by itself when your conversation matches.

A Claude app, Chat or Cowork

  1. Download monthly-progress-report.skill below.
  2. Open Claude, go to Settings → Capabilities → Skills and upload the file.
  3. Start a conversation and attach your material. The skill loads itself on a match.

Quick reference

How to run it.

A month's material becomes a draft client or funder report: progress, programme position, decisions, risks with their movement, information flow — every statement traced, every gap visible. Assembled, never recalled.

Run it

Attach the month's material and last month's report:

Draft the monthly report for March from these minutes, visit notes and the RFI log.
Monthly update for the funder — here's the programme and the site photos.
What changed since last month's report? Use both.
What can't we report on this month?

Last month's report roughly doubles the value of the output. Without it the risk movement column and the decision-age column cannot be produced — and those are what the report is for.

The report

SectionWhat it holds
Period & basisThe window, the assembly date, and every source with its date. A report built from two photos and a WhatsApp thread says so here.
HeadlineThree to five sentences, only facts evidenced below. Written last.
ProgressComplete / in progress / not started, in the programme's own terms, each with source and date.
Programme positionMilestones in the programme's own names and dates. Never a derived forecast.
DecisionsTaken (who, when, what it unblocks) and outstanding (how long, what it blocks).
Risks & issuesStatus, movement since last month, owner, mitigation as stated by its owner.
Information flowRFIs, instructions, submittals: raised, answered, outstanding, oldest.
GapsWhat the report could not cover and why. Never a footnote.

Provenance tags:

TagMeaning
[minuted]From minutes, with meeting reference and date.
[observed]From a site visit record, with visit reference and date.
[reported]Someone stated it. Attributed to them.
[logged]From a register — RFI, instruction, submittal, change.
[programme]From the supplied programme, with revision and date.
[not evidenced]Included because its absence would mislead. Visibly flagged.

"The contractor reports the roof is watertight" and "the roof was observed watertight on 14 March" are different statements with different consequences. A report that blurs them is the document someone quotes back at you.

What it will not do — by design

  • State a progress percentage nobody stated. No estimating from photographs, no inferring from activity, no ranges.
  • Assess delay, criticality, float or entitlement, or forecast completion. Those have their own methodologies and their own specialists.
  • Certify, value or comment on payment.
  • Promote [reported] to [observed].
  • Close a risk because nobody mentioned it this month. It carries forward unchanged, flagged as not reviewed.
  • Reconcile two conflicting figures. Both appear, attributed.
  • Write reassurance. "Progress remains satisfactory" with no evidenced basis is the sentence that gets quoted in a dispute.
  • Carry material from outside the period without saying so.
  • Characterise anyone's performance — contractor, consultant or client.
  • Issue. Every output is a draft for your review and signature.

Work the output

  1. Check the basis section first. If the evidence is thin, the report should look thin — do not let the headline outrun it.
  2. Review every [not evidenced] line. Each one is either a gap to close or a sentence to delete.
  3. Work the outstanding-decisions age column with the client. It is the column that changes behaviour.
  4. Check that no risk silently disappeared against last month. The skill carries them forward, but only if you gave it last month's report.
  5. Rewrite the headline in your own voice before issuing — it is yours to sign, and the body has to support every word of it.
  6. Keep the gaps section in the issued version. Deleting it is how next month's report inherits a hole nobody remembers.

Remember: the report protects you in exactly the places it refuses to be confident. A visible gap costs you a conversation; a confident sentence with no evidence behind it costs considerably more.

Triggering

When Claude reaches for it.

This is the description Claude itself reads to decide whether the skill applies. It is worth knowing, because it tells you exactly how to phrase the ask — and what the skill will decline.

monthly-progress-report

Turn a month's project material — site visit notes, minutes, RFI and instruction logs, programme extracts, photographs, correspondence — into a draft monthly progress report for a client or funder: period summary, progress against the stated programme, decisions taken and outstanding, risks with their movement since last month, and an issues list — every statement traced to a source, every figure carried verbatim, and every gap visible rather than smoothed. Use this WHENEVER someone asks to "write the monthly report", "draft the progress report", "monthly update for the client", "report for the funder", "what happened this month", "pull the month together", or runs a reporting cycle. Do NOT use it to assess delay or entitlement, to certify payment or valuation, to state progress percentages it was not given, or to forecast completion.
