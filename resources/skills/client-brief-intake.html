Quick reference

How to run it.

Turns a client brief into a traceable Excel register: requirements with IDs, a schedule of accommodation, a client query sheet, conflicts, and a design research agenda. Not a summariser — an audit baseline.

Run it

Attach the brief (PDF / Word / pasted email) and ask:

Process this client brief into a requirements register.
Rev C attached with the Rev B register — what changed?        (revision mode)
Turn on research mode and resolve statutory items with cited sources.

The workbook

TabWhat it holds
Read meSource doc, legend, Stated/Implied/Assumed counts. Always first.
RegisterOne row per requirement: REQ ID, category, source ref, provenance.
AccommodationSpaces, areas, basis, formula totals vs the brief's envelope.
QueriesClient-ready question sheet; yellow cells for their answers.
Conflicts & risksContradictions and envelope mismatches. Logged, never resolved.
Design implicationsWhat each requirement forces you to decide; research agenda.
Revision diffRev-to-rev changes with commercial implication. (Revision mode.)

Provenance — trust nothing before checking its colour:

TagMeaning
Stated (black)The brief says it. Source ref + verbatim fragment given.
Implied (amber fill)Follows necessarily from a stated item; basis noted.
Assumed (red)No basis in the brief. Named assumption; auto-copied to Queries.

A 40% red register is a finding about the brief, not a defect in the output. The dangerous register is the all-black one where half was invented.

IDs: REQ-<CAT>-<n> — ACC PER STAT COM OPS DES ASP. Permanent, never renumbered: they anchor revision diffs and later drawing audits.

What it will not do — by design

  • Invent numbers. Missing areas stay [not stated].
  • Quote standards from memory. Documents are named to consult, marked [to verify]; findings need a retrieved citation.
  • Resolve contradictions. Both versions logged with sources; the choice is yours.
  • Convert units or basis silently. sqm/sq ft and NIA/GIA mismatches are flagged, never reconciled.
  • Design. No massing, no test-fits, no "this suggests a courtyard".
  • Declare compliance. Statutory rows flag where a check is needed — verdicts come from drawings.
  • Web-search the client or site unless you ask; no client names in filenames.
  • Sign itself off. Everything is draft until the architect reviews it.

Work the output

  1. Read me tab — the Assumed count tells you in 10 seconds how much is really the client's instruction.
  2. Filter Register by provenance; review every red row before anything else.
  3. Send the Queries tab to the client early — it is formatted to go as-is.
  4. Take Conflicts & risks to the next client meeting; contradictions are cheapest before anyone designs against them.
  5. Allocate Design implications by research type: Statutory / Technical / Client / Cost / Site / Precedent / Market.
  6. On a revised brief: run revision mode, read the diff tab with the fee letter open. Brief drift = unbilled scope.

Remember: extraction is a strong first pass, not a substitute for reading — spot-check against sections you know. A thin brief correctly produces a thin register and a long query sheet. Test drive: run it on the Halden Wharf sample brief and score against its defect key.

Triggering

When Claude reaches for it.

This is the description Claude itself reads to decide whether the skill applies. It is worth knowing, because it tells you exactly how to phrase the ask — and what the skill will decline.

client-brief-intake

Turn a client brief into a traceable requirements register, a schedule of accommodation, a client query sheet, and a conflict/risk log — as an editable .xlsx — with every line tagged Stated / Implied / Assumed against a verbatim source. Use this WHENEVER an architect, urban designer or design manager supplies a client brief, employer's requirements, project brief, design brief, developer's brief, feasibility brief, or competition brief (as .pdf, .docx, email, or pasted text) and wants it "processed", "unpacked", "interrogated", "turned into a schedule of accommodation", "checked for gaps", "summarised for the team", or "compared against the last version". Also trigger on "what's missing from this brief", "write me a query sheet for the client", "what changed in brief v2", or any request to build a requirements register or brief-compliance baseline. Do NOT use for checking a *drawing* against regulations (that is a compliance-audit task), and do NOT use it to design, test-fit, or propose massing.
