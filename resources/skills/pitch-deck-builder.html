Quick reference

How to run it.

Turns a raw project pack into a client-facing pitch deck (.pptx) for a C-suite audience: brief interpretation, three design moves tied to business outcomes, methodology by RIBA stage with costs surfaced, team and experience. Everything on a slide traces to the pack — gaps are flagged, never filled.

Run it

Attach the project pack (plus template, CVs, case studies, fee data if you have them) and ask:

Build the pitch deck from this project pack.
Populate our attached master with this pack — interview version, 10 slides.
Just give me the slide-by-slide copy, no file.                (text-only mode)

It confirms what it received, asks up to five questions (say "use defaults" to skip), and returns the deck plus a short overview and flag list in chat.

The deck

PartWhat it holds
Exec summaryThe whole pitch on one slide: understand / propose / why us.
Understanding the briefClient priorities in their language; success metrics.
Three design movesNamed moves, each tied to a business outcome. Proposed for your approval — not decided.
Methodology + programmeRIBA stages: purpose, deliverables, your client's decision gates, cost implication; risks to the date named.
Team & experienceOnly people, projects, stats and awards from the supplied material, correctly attributed.
Investment summaryStage × deliverable × fee. No fee data → structure with [TBC — insert from rate card].
[image: …] boxesLabelled placeholders stating exactly which photo/diagram to drop in; rights queries appended to the label.
INTERNAL — flagsEvery gap, TBC, unverified claim and permission to clear. Remove before issue.

A deck with visible TBCs is a draft doing its job. The dangerous deck is the polished one where the fee, the stat and the award were guessed.

What it will not do — by design

  • Invent fees, stats, dates or people. Empty slots become labelled TBCs.
  • Generate or fetch images. Every image is a captioned placeholder box; your photography is your evidence.
  • Launder credentials. Previous-practice work is attributed; "check with QS" stats stay off client-facing copy; unsure shortlisted-vs-won claims say neither.
  • Use informal intel on slides. Hearsay about the client shapes emphasis only — it is never quoted as their stated priority.
  • Quote policy or standards from memory. Named document + [to verify].
  • Resolve contradictions. Two budget figures → neutral copy, both figures flagged.
  • Decide the design. The three moves are framings for you to approve; no massing, storeys or materials beyond the pack.
  • Web-search the client or site unless you ask; no client names or fees in filenames.
  • Call itself finished. Draft for internal review until you sign it off.

Work the output

  1. Read the flags slide first — it is your review agenda; resolve the top item before polishing anything.
  2. Approve or redirect the win theme and the three moves — everything else hangs off them.
  3. Replace every [image: …] box with real photography; clear the rights queries as you go.
  4. Insert fees from the rate card; check the stage split matches your appointment offer.
  5. Verify every stat and award on the experience slides against records, not memory.
  6. Apply the studio master if the deck was built on the neutral default, then delete the internal flags slide before issue.

Remember: the deck argues only as hard as your pack allows — feed it your best case studies and real fee data and it gets sharper. Test drive: run it on the Tannery Yard sample pack and score against its defect key.

Triggering

When Claude reaches for it.

This is the description Claude itself reads to decide whether the skill applies. It is worth knowing, because it tells you exactly how to phrase the ask — and what the skill will decline.

pitch-deck-builder

Turn a practice's raw project pack — opportunity notes, practice profile, project list, team bios, fee data if any — into a client-facing pitch deck as a real .pptx, written for a C-suite audience: brief interpretation up front, three key design moves tied to business outcomes, methodology by RIBA stage with cost implications surfaced, then team and relevant experience. Use this WHENEVER an architect or practice director asks for a "pitch deck", "bid deck", "interview deck", "capability presentation", "competition interview slides", "deck for the client meeting", or says "turn this project pack into slides", "build the pitch for [opportunity]", "we're pitching next week — make a start on the deck". Also trigger when a template (.pptx/.potx) is supplied with source material for a BD audience. Do NOT use for design review, planning committee or public consultation presentations, for the written bid document (rfp-response-builder), or for calculating what the fee should be — this skill never invents fee figures.
