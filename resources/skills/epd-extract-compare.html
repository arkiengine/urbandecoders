What it takes, what you get.

It takes

Environmental Product Declarations as supplied files

You get

Declared unit, module scope, service life, standard, PCR, validity and impacts as printed; a comparability verdict per pair before any comparison; a like-for-like table; and what data would be needed to compare the rest.

It will not

Supply EPD data from memory, source EPDs, declare a winner, normalise between declared units, or let `[not declared]` read as zero.

Quick reference

How to run it.

Declared data pulled out of supplied EPDs and laid side by side only where the comparison is fair. Every mismatch that makes two EPDs non-comparable is flagged before any comparison is shown.

Run it

Attach the EPDs:

Extract and compare these three insulation EPDs.
Are these two actually comparable? The supplier says theirs is lower.
Pull the declared data out of these EPDs into a table for the spec.
Which of these are still valid, and what would we need to compare the rest properly?

Say what the comparison is for — specification, a certification credit, a client question. The caveats are written against that use.

The output

PartWhat it holds
Extraction tableOne column per EPD: operator, number, dates, standard, PCR, manufacturer, type, declared unit, modules, RSL, impacts as printed.
Comparability verdictPer pair: comparable / comparable with conditions / not comparable, reason named. Before any comparison.
Validity checkCurrent, expired, expiring soon.
Like-for-like tableOnly the subset that genuinely compares, on their shared basis.
Normalisation requirementWhat product data would be needed to compare the rest, and who supplies it.
CaveatsWhat a specifier must know before using any of it.

Tags: [as printed] · [not declared] · [unreadable] · [expired] · [to verify].

[not declared] is never shown as zero, a dash, or a blank that reads as zero. A module nobody assessed is not a module with no impact. That substitution is the most consequential silent error in this whole domain.

Why two real, published, verified numbers still don't compare

  • Different declared units — per m², per kg, per m³, per "1 m² at 100 mm with a 60-year RSL".
  • Different module scope — A1–A3 against A1–A5 is not a fair fight.
  • Different EPD type — product-specific against industry-average.
  • Different PCR, standard version or geography.
  • Expired — an EPD past its validity date is not data, it is history.

What it will not do — by design

  • Supply EPD data or a GWP figure from memory. If it was not in a supplied document, it does not exist here.
  • Find or source EPDs unless you ask and supply the route.
  • Declare a winner or rank by impact. Products are chosen on performance, availability, cost, buildability and appearance too — none of which is in an EPD.
  • Normalise between declared units. Normalising needs density, thickness, coverage and service life — specification questions, not arithmetic ones.
  • Sum modules the EPD presents separately, or merge biogenic carbon into GWP where the EPD separates them.
  • Present an expired EPD as current.
  • Compare across declared units with a caveat. A caveated wrong comparison still gets quoted without the caveat.
  • State what a standard or PCR requires from memory.

Work the output

  1. Read the comparability verdict before the numbers. If the pair fails the gate, the numbers beside each other mean nothing.
  2. Supply the normalisation inputs yourself — specified thickness, density, coverage, agreed service life — then the arithmetic runs with your inputs attributed to you.
  3. Check EPD type on anything heading into a specification. An industry average does not describe the product you will actually buy.
  4. Watch the validity dates. Re-run before any submission that relies on them.
  5. Keep [not declared] visible in anything you pass on. The moment it becomes a blank cell in someone's summary, it becomes a zero.
  6. Treat the extraction as product data, not building data. For a building-level answer use embodied-carbon-takeoff, and give the assessor these EPDs.

Remember: the supplier quoting the lowest number is usually quoting a different unit, a shorter module scope, or an expired declaration. The gate exists to find out which.

Triggering

When Claude reaches for it.

This is the description Claude itself reads to decide whether the skill applies. It is worth knowing, because it tells you exactly how to phrase the ask — and what the skill will decline.

epd-extract-compare

Extract the declared data from supplied Environmental Product Declarations and lay several side by side honestly: declared unit, scope and modules, reference service life, standard and PCR, validity dates, programme operator, and the declared impact figures as printed — with every mismatch that makes two EPDs non-comparable flagged before any comparison is shown. Use this WHENEVER someone supplies EPDs and asks to "compare these EPDs", "which product has lower embodied carbon", "pull the GWP out of these", "are these comparable", "extract the EPD data into a table", "check this EPD is still valid", or is selecting between products on environmental grounds. Do NOT use it to supply EPD or carbon data from memory, to find EPDs (it reads what you supply), to declare a winner or recommend a product, to normalise figures between different declared units, or to perform a building-level assessment (embodied-carbon-takeoff).
