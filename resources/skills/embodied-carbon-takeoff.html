Quick reference

How to run it.

A model export or schedule becomes an auditable quantity register for a carbon assessment: scope, exclusions, module coverage, quantities with their source, and an empty sourced slot for every factor. Prepares the assessor's input. Supplies no factor, calculates no total.

Run it

Attach the model export, schedule or drawings:

Prepare an embodied carbon takeoff from this Revit schedule export for Stage 3.
Structure these quantities for our LCA consultant — what are we missing?
What's out of scope, and what would need to be in for a planning submission?
Which lines can't be priced because the material description isn't specific enough?

Name the assessment methodology, the required modules and the element classification. Without them the register still gets built on a generic classification — and will probably need restructuring later.

The register

PartWhat it holds
Basis of takeoffEvery source with revision and date; the methodology (or [not stated]); the classification; the stage.
Scope & exclusionsWhat is in, out, or [to verify] — written before any quantity, because quantities are meaningless without it.
Module coverageWhich life-cycle modules this takeoff supports, in the methodology's own naming.
Quantity registerRef · Element · Classification · Material description verbatim · Quantity · Unit · Source · Confidence · Modules covered · Factor [to be supplied] · Factor source [to be supplied].
Assumption logEvery incomplete material description and what was assumed to keep the line usable.
Gaps listElements the supplied material does not quantify at all.
Questions for the assessorScope boundaries, service lives, study period, sequestration, database vintage.

Confidence tags: [modelled] · [scheduled] · [measured] · [estimated] · [not quantified] · [to verify].

No totals. No factors. No kgCO₂e. A number in this register is a defect.

Why the scope section comes first

Two assessments of the same building routinely differ by a factor of two, and almost never because the factors differed. They differ because one included substructure and the other did not, or one assessed A1–A3 and the other A1–A5 and the comparison was made as though they were the same thing.

What it will not do — by design

  • Supply a factor, GWP figure, density, service life or benchmark. Not from memory, not as a placeholder, not "to be refined later". A placeholder number survives into reports.
  • Calculate a total, a per-m² rate, or any intermediate product.
  • Compare against a target or say whether a scheme is performing well.
  • Recommend a material or a reduction strategy. The register is input; design response is yours and the assessor's.
  • Convert units using an assumed density. If a conversion is needed, the density is a question for the assessor, recorded as one.
  • Call a line complete when the material description cannot select a factor. "Concrete" is not a material description for carbon purposes.
  • State what a methodology or certification scheme requires from memory.

Work the output

  1. Agree the scope and exclusions statement with the assessor before anyone works the quantities. It is the only section that makes the result comparable to anything.
  2. Audit the classification mapping — model categories rarely align with an assessment classification, and the mapping is judgement, deliberately left visible rather than inherited.
  3. Close the [to verify] material descriptions with the specification. Cement replacement, steel route, timber treatment: each moves the answer materially.
  4. Expect a long gaps list at early stages. An early register with nothing missing has been padded.
  5. Send the questions-for-the-assessor list with the fee enquiry. Service lives and study period change results more than most design decisions.
  6. Re-run at each stage and keep the scope statement constant, or say loudly that it changed. A moving scope is why carbon figures appear to improve.

Remember: the multiplication is trivial and the boundary is everything. This register exists so that whoever does the multiplying can show exactly what they multiplied — and what they did not.

Triggering

When Claude reaches for it.

This is the description Claude itself reads to decide whether the skill applies. It is worth knowing, because it tells you exactly how to phrase the ask — and what the skill will decline.

embodied-carbon-takeoff

Turn a model export, quantity schedule or drawing takeoff into a structured embodied carbon quantity register — elements mapped to a stated assessment structure, with quantities, units, material descriptions, the life-cycle modules each line would cover, and an empty, sourced slot for every carbon factor — plus a scope and exclusions statement and a gaps list. It never supplies a carbon factor, a GWP figure or a benchmark, and never reports a total. Use this WHENEVER someone asks to "do an embodied carbon takeoff", "prepare the quantities for a WLCA", "what do we need for the carbon assessment", "structure this for the LCA consultant", "which elements are missing from the carbon scope", or is preparing a whole-life carbon submission. Do NOT use it to calculate embodied carbon, to supply or estimate factors or benchmarks, to compare a scheme against a target, or to replace a qualified assessor — it prepares their input.
