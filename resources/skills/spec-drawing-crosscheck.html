Quick reference

How to run it.

Every specified item traced to the drawings, every drawn item traced to a clause, orphans both ways, conflicts quoted side by side. Makes the disagreement visible. Never resolves it, never writes the missing clause.

Run it

Attach the specification, the drawings and any schedules:

Cross-check the spec against the drawings before this goes out to tender.
What's drawn but not specified? The contractor's queries are all in one area.
Check the door and ironmongery schedules against the spec sections.
Which performance clauses have no detail that could demonstrate them?

Say what the issue is for, and flag it if spec and drawings are at different revisions. The report leads with that, because otherwise half the findings are artefacts of the gap.

Match statuses:

StatusMeaning
matchedIn both, descriptions do not contradict.
conflictIn both, descriptions disagree. Both quoted.
spec onlyA clause with no drawn or scheduled counterpart.
drawing onlyDrawn or scheduled, no clause.
partialIn both, but one is materially less specific.
[verify manually]Unresolvable from what was supplied.

partial is the status that earns its keep. A clause for "proprietary metal rainscreen" against three visibly different drawn cladding conditions is not a match — and calling it one is how a tender query becomes a variation.

The four gaps it hunts

  1. Specified, not drawn — a clause for a product on no sheet.
  2. Drawn, not specified — a hatch, note or schedule row with no clause. The contractor prices the minimum.
  3. Two descriptions — schedule says FD30S, spec says FD60. Both are contractual.
  4. Performance with nothing to demonstrate it — an acoustic or thermal requirement that no drawn junction resolves.

What it will not do — by design

  • Write clauses. Not even obvious ones, not even as a suggestion. A gap is reported as a gap.
  • Resolve a conflict or hint which reading is correct — not even to note that one is "probably" a typo.
  • Judge whether a specified product is suitable, compliant or available. That is a specifier's judgement with professional consequences.
  • Supply a standard, rating, U-value or product datum from memory. It names what the documents name and marks the rest [to verify].
  • Check against regulations. A coordination finding is not a compliance finding and the two are never blurred.
  • Normalise wording between spec and drawings. The difference in wording is often the finding.
  • Declare a set coordinated. It says what it checked and what agreed.

Work the output

  1. Read the revision-gap note first. If spec and drawings are four revisions apart, fix that before working the findings.
  2. Close drawing only items before tender — unspecified drawn work is priced at the contractor's discretion, and the discretion is not yours.
  3. Send conflicts to whoever has design authority over that element. The report deliberately refuses to choose, so somebody must.
  4. Work the [TBC] list in the order given — it is ordered by what it blocks, not by section.
  5. Treat an unresolved performance clause as a detailing instruction, not a specification one. The clause is fine; the junction is missing.
  6. Re-run after the next issue of either document. Coordination is a state, not an achievement.

Remember: the contractor will find every gap in this report during the tender period. The only question is whether you find them first, while they are still coordination and not yet money.

Triggering

When Claude reaches for it.

This is the description Claude itself reads to decide whether the skill applies. It is worth knowing, because it tells you exactly how to phrase the ask — and what the skill will decline.

spec-drawing-crosscheck

Cross-check a specification against drawings and schedules: every specified item traced to where it appears on the drawings, every drawn or scheduled item traced to its specification clause, with orphans both ways, conflicting descriptions quoted side by side, performance requirements that no drawing can demonstrate flagged, and [TBC] items listed by consequence. Use this WHENEVER someone supplies a spec plus drawings or schedules and asks to "cross-check the spec against the drawings", "does the spec match the drawings", "find the gaps before tender", "what's specified but not drawn", "what's drawn but not specified", "check the door schedule against the spec", or runs a pre-tender or pre-construction coordination check. Do NOT use it to write specification clauses, to judge whether a specified product is suitable or compliant, to supply standards, performance figures or product data from memory, or to check drawings against regulations (part-m-drawing-check).
