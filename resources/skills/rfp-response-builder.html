Quick reference

How to run it.

Turns an RFP/ITT plus the practice's bid materials into an editable .docx response draft: compliance matrix, tailored answers in the client's own order, [TBC] wherever the materials don't support a claim, and a before-issue checklist. Not the incoming-tender digest — that's Bid Summariser, which runs first.

Run it

Attach the tender pack and the practice's materials in the same message, then ask:

Draft our tender response to this RFP.
Answer this ITT — qualifications pack, CVs and rate card attached.
Draft the quality responses against the scored criteria; leave the fee as structure only.
Bid summary already done — build the response from it and the pack.

Attach at minimum the RFP and a qualifications pack. Also useful: CVs, case-study sheets, rate card, policies, insurance certificates. Whatever is missing is not guessed — the affected sections come back as gaps.

Expect an intake check, an RFP decode, then 5–8 numbered questions with defaults. Reply "use defaults" to proceed.

The document

SectionWhat it holds
Cover sheetProject, client, deadline, DRAFT banner — not for issue until the checklist clears.
RFP snapshotDeadlines, delivery method, limits, scored criteria + weightings (summed and checked), pass/fail criteria, inferred client priorities (labelled as inference).
Clarification questionsNumbered, quoting both clauses of each contradiction. Ready to send before the question deadline.
Compliance matrixEvery requirement R-nn → clause → weighting → answering section → status. Pass/fail rows first.
Response draftCover letter · understanding · methodology (longest where the weighting says so) · experience · team · social value · quality/risk/H&S · fee · programme.
Before-issue checklistPass/fail risks first, then [TBC]s by tag, assumptions, formalities. Each an action with an owner.

Tags — nothing untagged is a claim:

TagRoutes to
[TBC — supply]A fact the practice holds but the pack doesn't: contract values, dates, referees, PI figure, certificate numbers. Someone fetches it.
[TBC — decision]A choice only the practice can make: the fee, the named team, an equivalence argument, positioning. Someone decides it.
[to verify]An external figure, standard or policy named from context but not checked at source. Someone verifies it.
[Boilerplate — trim/tailor]Reused pack text that still needs a human pass.

A draft that is honestly 15% placeholder is the correct output. A draft that is 100% confident and 15% fiction is a professional hazard — and it surfaces after award, in front of the client.

What it will not do — by design

  • Invent credentials. No fabricated referees, awards, contract values, completion dates, accreditations, insurance figures or client names. The test: could you evidence this sentence if the evaluator asked?
  • Upgrade the pack's own words. "Experience of listed buildings" never becomes "conservation-accredited"; "aligned with ISO 9001 principles" never becomes "ISO 9001 certified".
  • Set the fee. Structure, basis, assumptions and exclusions — yes. The number only from your explicit input, shown with its arithmetic.
  • Declare the submission compliant. The matrix maps requirements to answers; it does not certify that pass/fail criteria are met. Certificates do that.
  • Resolve RFP contradictions. Both clause refs recorded, question drafted, tighter reading drafted to. You decide what to ask.
  • Drop a requirement to fit a page limit silently. Cuts are declared and the matrix is re-checked.
  • Quote standards or statutory periods from memory. Named to check, marked [to verify].
  • Web-search the client, site or competitors unless you ask; no client names in output filenames.

Work the output

  1. Before-issue checklist first, not last. Anything marked pass/fail is an exclusion risk — clear those before a word of prose is polished.
  2. Send the clarification questions today; the question deadline expires well before the tender does.
  3. Walk the compliance matrix with the RFP open. Confirm each target section answers the question rather than mentioning the topic — the skill re-checks, but only a human can judge sufficiency.
  4. Chase every [TBC — supply] as a batch: one email to the person who holds the certificates, one to whoever holds the case-study data.
  5. Take the [TBC — decision] list to a director — fee, named team, whether to make any equivalence argument. These are the commercial calls.
  6. Re-read every [Boilerplate — trim/tailor] passage against this client. Reused text that survives unedited is what makes a bid read generic.
  7. Do the page-count arithmetic yourself against the limit you confirmed, then re-check the matrix if anything was cut.

Remember: the skill drafts, the practice submits. Nothing here is signed off, and the checklist is the condition of issue, not a suggestion. Test drive: run it on the Redgate Street Baths sample RFP and materials pack, and score against the defect key.

Triggering

When Claude reaches for it.

This is the description Claude itself reads to decide whether the skill applies. It is worth knowing, because it tells you exactly how to phrase the ask — and what the skill will decline.

rfp-response-builder

Turn an incoming RFP / ITT / tender invitation plus the practice's standard bid materials (qualifications pack, CVs, case studies, rate card) into a compliant, client-specific tender response draft as an editable .docx — with a compliance matrix tracing every scored question to a response section, [TBC] placeholders wherever the materials don't support a claim, and a before-issue checklist of everything a human must close before submission. Use this WHENEVER someone supplies tender documents and practice materials and asks to "respond to this RFP", "draft our tender response", "write the bid", "build the proposal", "answer this ITT", "put the submission together", "draft the quality responses", "assemble our return for [project]", or "draft the methodology against the scored criteria". Do NOT use for digesting or deciding on an incoming tender — that is bid-summariser, which runs FIRST and feeds this. Do NOT use for client brief intake, appointment terms review, or marketing decks.
