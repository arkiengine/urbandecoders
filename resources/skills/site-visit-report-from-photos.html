Quick reference

How to run it.

Turns site photos plus two or three lines of narration per photo into a structured site visit report (.docx): observations grouped by location, a provenance tag and a photo reference on every line, follow-up actions with owners, a full photo schedule, and an unresolved-queries list. Not a write-up — an evidential record that has to survive being quoted.

Run it

Upload the photos (or a photo manifest) plus your notes, and ask:

Write up my site visit from these photos and notes.
Site visit report — studio template attached, use its structure.
Here's SVR 06 as well — carry forward the open actions.        (previous-report mode)

Narrating: two or three lines per photo — location/element, what was inspected, what was found, follow-up and owner if agreed. Photos with no narration still get captioned, but strictly from the frame.

The report

SectionWhat it holds
HeaderProject, ref, visit no., date/time, weather, RIBA stage, contract, attendees, prepared by. Unstated fields say TBC, never a guess. Status: DRAFT for architect review.
PurposeOne paragraph, including the visit-type caveat: periodic visual inspection, not a survey and not supervision.
Areas inspected / not inspectedTwo lists. The second one is the point — silence about coverage implies you covered everything.
Observations (O-nn)Grouped by location. Each: observation in observation-language, provenance tag(s), photo ref(s), linked action.
Follow-up actions (A-nn)Action, owner, due, linked obs/photos. Only actions the notes state were agreed.
Photo scheduleEvery photo: ref, filename, date/time (or not recorded), subject, linked obs, anomalies. Nothing is dropped.
Unresolved queriesContradictions, unverified dates, illegible frames, TBCs, notes-only items, possible instructions. Your pre-issue worklist.
Limitations & sign-offVisual, from accessible positions, on the stated date, no opening-up. Names you as the author who must review and issue.

Provenance — read the tag before you read the sentence:

TagMeaning
[visible]Directly observable in the referenced photo(s). The default.
[notes]Stated in your narration, not verifiable from any photo. Rendered as "reported… [notes — not photographed]".
[inferred]One step beyond the frame, with the observable basis named. Stops at consistent with — never a cause, never a verdict.

Where your notes assert a cause, the report keeps the two apart: "Tide-mark staining to base of wall [visible, P01]; notes attribute this to DPC failure [notes — cause not verifiable from photograph]." You may well be right. The report just won't say so on your behalf.

Refs: P01… for photos (timestamp order), O-01… for observations, A-01… for actions. Stable, so an RFI, a snagging list or the next visit can cite O-04 and mean the same thing. Original filenames travel with every P-ref — that is how anyone finds the image again in three years.

What it will not do — by design

  • Diagnose or condemn. No causes, no severity gradings, no condition ratings. "Cracking observed", never "structural movement".
  • Instruct. No "open up", "remove and replace", "rejected", "accepted". If your notes record you saying it on site, it goes in as attributed reported speech plus a query asking whether a written instruction follows.
  • Certify. Not completion, not workmanship, not compliance, not safety, not that the site matches the drawings.
  • Quote regulations from memory. Approved Document named, figure [to verify] — including when the figure came from your own notes.
  • Adjudicate safety. An unprotected edge is recorded factually and referred to the principal contractor. CDM duties are the PC's; the report neither clears the site nor declares a breach.
  • Double-count. Two angles of one crack = one observation, two photo refs. Nor will it over-merge: similar staining on two elevations stays two observations.
  • Compare across dates. Different angle, distance and light don't establish that a crack grew. Both dates recorded, the question raised.
  • Adopt a missing date. No timestamp means not recorded, not the visit date.
  • Invent. Attendees, weather, owners, due dates, defects. TBC is a professional answer.
  • Drop a photo. Unmatched frames still appear in the schedule.
  • Web-search the site, client or contractor, or put client names in filenames.
  • Sign itself off. Draft on its face until you review, amend and issue it.

Work the output

  1. Read Unresolved queries first, before the observations. It is short and it is where the risk is.
  2. Settle every note-vs-photo contradiction from memory while the visit is fresh — that window closes in about a day.
  3. Check anything tagged [notes] or [inferred]. If you can't stand behind it in two years, cut it or re-photograph next visit.
  4. Fill the header TBCs and the actions table TBCs — an action with no owner and no date is not an action.
  5. Decide on anything the queries flag as a possible instruction: issue it properly under the contract, or take it out.
  6. Check the frames flagged as illegible, undated or containing an identifiable person before the report leaves the project team.
  7. Delete the DRAFT status line only when you are content to have every sentence quoted back at you. Then issue.

Remember: the report covers one visit, from accessible positions, and only what was photographed or noted — it cannot know what you saw and didn't record. A thin photo set correctly produces a short report and a long query list; that is the accurate answer, not a gap to fill with prose. Test drive: run it on the Rushmere Court SVR 07 sample manifest and score against its defect key.

Triggering

When Claude reaches for it.

This is the description Claude itself reads to decide whether the skill applies. It is worth knowing, because it tells you exactly how to phrase the ask — and what the skill will decline.

site-visit-report-from-photos

Turn a set of site-visit photos plus the architect's brief narration into a structured, editable site visit report (.docx) — observations grouped by location, every observation carrying a photo reference and a provenance tag (visible / notes / inferred), follow-up actions with owners, and an unresolved queries section. Use this WHENEVER an architect, technologist or design manager uploads site photos (or a photo manifest or contact sheet) and asks to "write up my site visit", "turn these photos into a report", produce a "site visit report", "site inspection report", "observation report", "progress report from photos", "CA visit record" or "snag write-up from photos" — or pastes photo-by-photo notes and asks for them to be formatted into the studio's report template. Do NOT use for condition surveys with condition ratings, for diagnosing defects or certifying workmanship, for checking drawings against regulations, or for valuations and payment certification.
